# Forest Spring · LayoutVersion 1 · art revision 2

The footprint (39 × 32 × 55), gameplay coordinates, B4 ownership, version 0 hashes and surface placement rules remain those of the first checkpoint. This is an art revision of the unsubmitted LayoutVersion 1, not a new layout migration. Source revision v001 remains preserved.

The west root chamber and east two-key treasury now have high broken walls, narrow stone-mullioned windows, incomplete arches and mostly open roofs. Their low exterior vines do not make a climbable ladder to the roof. The secret wall and key door still provide the only ordinary ground entries. The shrine and map courts retain their open fronts and separate focus spaces.

Root centrelines are Catmull–Rom curves sampled below voxel size and connected by single-axis voxel steps, rather than long vertical-then-horizontal elbows. Lower trunks widen to 2×2 and spread small root feet at ground level; upper arms taper to a single block. Attached asymmetric crowns are several layers thick, with ragged low edges. All vanilla leaves are persistent=true. SpiritLeavesBlock is an ordinary non-decaying Block, not vanilla LeavesBlock.

Piers vary from low foundations to fractured tall uprights, with small fallen pieces. Moss, grass and exposed soil enter the paving; the pale worn centre path still points towards the storm altar. A perturbed clay-lined pool boundary adds coves and gravel shallows on the outer banks. The reward island's approach gap and the deep return channel remain protected by the original dark-bridge navigation tests.

Sap knots are limited to seven actual blocks: one entry signature, one at each side court, and four around the spring mechanic (entry, lamp, two submerged bed inlays). No ordinary stone pier carries a knot. Fixed verdant masonry inlays, luminous foliage and thin lichen supply the baseline. Decorative fruit does not count: the runtime light test removes containers and sets all cave-vine berries=false before scanning actual standable surfaces, including broken half-slab tops. Focus and mechanic approaches must reach block light 7; all other standable surfaces must reach 1.

The template generator validates water support and route clearance before packaging. Runtime rotation, ownership, keystone, real Bash launch, dark-bridge bypass/retreat, rain/runoff/aquifer and foundation regressions remain unchanged in scope. Permanent-light coverage is deliberately stronger than revision 1 because harvested berries are excluded.

The final revision thickens lower crown-bearing trunks and adds three short branches per crown. Broken wall tops vary in broad collapses rather than alternating teeth, with small rubble slabs at their feet. A graded grass skirt joins the complete authored footprint to natural ground. It fits both the deck and the surrounding terrain with a one-block-per-column slope envelope, continuing beyond four columns only where necessary, to a maximum of eight. Remaining outer cliffs receive a mossy retaining face and a real 1.5-block guard lip only on the steep columns; the five-wide saved entrance approach never receives a lip. The chunk envelope and generated-chunk reservation include the expanded skirt and its shallow subsurface support. The legacy template receives neither grading nor additional foundations.

The foundation audit found eight grounded columns beyond the original garden outline: the entry piers at (13,3)/(25,3), and six flared root feet at (10,51), (28,51), (29,50), (30,49), (36,22), (37,21). Each now gets authored dirt from y0 through y4 beneath its unchanged y5 member and joins the bottom-DIRT footprint used by grading and carved-pocket support. Two elevated crown tips at (13,17,52)/(26,18,52) are attached overhangs, not ground feet; they do not get artificial pillars. The manifest records foundation extensions. Regression checks keep adjacent ground steps at 1, explicitly including columns four to five. Only downward cliffs at the eight-column limit may use the tested retaining face/lip fallback. Tests report guard columns as a fraction of the outer boundary, require a real cliff for every lip, continuous portal-pier support, and a no-ability walk from natural ground beyond the skirt through the saved approach to the entrance in all four rotations, without excluding failing coordinates.

The skirt shares the saved 45-column approach profile with the entrance piece, anticipating its stairs even over a carver dip. Retaining lips stand on masonry rather than grass to prevent later flower decoration from replacing them. Prior-slice retaining stone remains a terrain-height source. Missing profile data does not change legacy layouts.

Because world generation defers neighbour-shape updates, flowers already occupying a lip cell are explicitly replaced (including tall-flower upper halves). A regression uses deferred shape updates; a normal live-level fixture would silently remove the unsupported flower and conceal this case.
